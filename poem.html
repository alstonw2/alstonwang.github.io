2006 · the move

A New Place

With a first line inspired by Li-Young Lee's “Persimmons”
Alston Wang
In first grade I told my teacher I was going to America, a decision I couldn't quite understand.
After all, we were well off. Dad was a bank manager and mom had her group of friends she played Mahjong with until the next morning. My sister was just beginning high school. And I was just about to start making my own friends. Yet, without many questions, we entered a new journey.
40,000 feet above ground, I pondered on the cold seat as I leaned on mom's shoulder. I knew she was excited. But I was afraid. With cup noodle, a Haagen Daz Cookies and cream ice cream, and an orange smoothie in front of me, I still couldn't find the appetite to enjoy the meal.
The first step felt the same. The same hard diamond patterned glass floor. Though fascinated by the buildings, nerves still bothered me. Jetlag, insomnia, and then a fever. It felt like the end of the world. That was the first time I saw dad let down a tear. I knew he was worried about me. But in that moment, I also knew that he was scared of this place just like I was.

Part of Remnant — a record of a life, kept while there is still someone to keep it.
